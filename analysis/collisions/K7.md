# K7: flag and bitfield collisions

## Summary

**367** headers change in the series; **224** of them exist in both trees and were scanned, **143** skipped
(**136** absent from sdm670, **7** absent from the series head — all other-architecture headers). **46** rows in
[`K7.tsv`](K7.tsv). The acceptance test passes: both known cases are found, and the third known case
(`skc_tx_queue_mapping` `int` → `unsigned short`) is found too, so the category scheme covers bit positions,
same-name value changes, enum renumbering and field widths.

**Beyond the two cases already in `LEAD-SYNTHESIS.md` §2, I found three more real fusions** — all live in all four
defconfigs:

1. **`VM_ARCH_2` / `VM_WIPEONFORK`, both `0x2000000`** in `include/linux/mm.h` (`a30605a54f3b:190`,
   `baa585f67e0e:215`). The series renames sdm670's spare arch bit to a real feature (`MADV_WIPEONFORK`).
   Keeping both gives one bit with two meanings; `mmflags.h` would then print the same bit under two names.
2. **`VM_LOWMEM` / `VM_FLUSH_RESET_PERMS`, both `0x100`** in `include/linux/vmalloc.h` (`a30605a54f3b:22`,
   `baa585f67e0e:26`). Same failure mode, `vm_struct.flags`.
3. **`SW_HPHL_OVERCURRENT` / `SW_MACHINE_COVER`, both `0x10`**, plus `SW_MAX` `0x20` → `0x10`, in
   `include/uapi/linux/input-event-codes.h` (`a30605a54f3b:824,830` / `baa585f67e0e:811,812`). This one is
   **uapi**: `drivers/input/evdev.c:813` sizes `dev->swbit` from `SW_MAX`, so a keep-both merge silently
   truncates the switch-event bit array and breaks the input ABI for userspace.

**The lead must check `VM_ARCH_2`/`VM_WIPEONFORK` first.** It is the same class as the two known cases, it is
unconditional in `mm.h` (compiled by every `CONFIG_MMU=y` arm64 build), and unlike `TIF_UPROBE` it is not
confined to an optional feature — the series actively *sets* the bit from `mm/madvise.c:99`.

`MPTCP` is absent from all four defconfigs (`# CONFIG_MPTCP is not set` in both `*_eur_open`, absent in the other
two; the script's resolver confirms `n` in all four after following selects). Nothing found here is behind
`CONFIG_MPTCP`. **The `sk_buff.cb` shrink (§2's fourth row) does not appear in my results at all** — sdm670 and the
series head both declare `char cb[48]`, so there is nothing to fix. §2's row is right that no change is needed.

### Notes on each, and the free values

**Row 1 — `TIF_*`.** Confirms §2 and the P3 fix. Bit 4 is `TIF_FSCHECK` in sdm670 and `TIF_UPROBE` in the
series. Free slots in the merged view of `thread_info.h`: indices **5 and 6** are unused in both trees (both
trees jump 4 → 7 at `TIF_NOHZ`), and 12–17, 25–28 are free too. **Use 5**, and add `_TIF_UPROBE` to
`_TIF_WORK_MASK` — sdm670's version at `:109-111` ends in `_TIF_FSCHECK`, the series' at `:114-116` in
`_TIF_UPROBE`; the merged mask needs **both**, or `TIF_FSCHECK` stops waking the task. `_TIF_FSCHECK` is at
`a30605a54f3b:106` and has no `_TIF_UPROBE` counterpart line to lose.
`confidence: high` — both defines read directly, and the free-index set is the union of both trees.

**Row 2 — `FAULT_FLAG_*`.** Confirms §2. `0x400` is taken in sdm670 by `FAULT_FLAG_PREFAULT_OLD` (`:294`, used at
`mm/filemap.c:2519,2521` and `mm/memory.c:3300`) and **is not in the series head at all** — the series dropped
that flag, so `0x400` is *not* free. `0x800` is unused in both trees. **Use `0x800`.** Also keep
`FAULT_FLAG_SPECULATIVE`, which has 9 use sites in `mm/memory.c` @ `a30605a54f3b`.
`confidence: high` — full value list read from both files, all `FAULT_FLAG_` users grepped.

**Row 3 — `VM_*` (new).** sdm670 defines `VM_ARCH_1 0x01000000` and `VM_ARCH_2 0x02000000` as spare
architecture bits; on arm64 the only in-tree consumer of `VM_ARCH_2` is the trace name table
(`include/trace/events/mmflags.h:132`, `__VM_ARCH_SPECIFIC_2 {VM_ARCH_2, "arch_2"}`; `VM_MPX` at `mm.h:238` is
x86-only). The series takes bit `0x02000000` for `VM_WIPEONFORK` (`MADV_WIPEONFORK`, set at
`baa585f67e0e:mm/madvise.c:99`, cleared `:102`, consumed in `kernel/fork.c:642-643,691`, and named at
`baa585f67e0e:include/trace/events/mmflags.h:167`). A keep-both merge means `arch_2` and `wipeonfork` name the
same bit, and `fs/proc/task_mmu.c:728` `[ilog2(VM_ARCH_1)] = "ar"` vs `baa585f67e0e:fs/proc/task_mmu.c:752`
`[ilog2(VM_WIPEONFORK)] = "wf"` fill the same array slot. **Move `VM_ARCH_2` to `0x800000`** (bit 23 is unused in
both trees) or drop it on arm64 — it has no arm64 user. **This is a P3 item §6c does not currently list.**
`confidence: high` — every claim is a direct read or grep at a cited SHA.

**Row 4 — `vmalloc.h` `VM_` (new).** Both `0x100` in `vm_struct.flags`. sdm670's `VM_LOWMEM` is used at
`arch/arm/mm/mmu.c:1540` (arm32 `map_lowmem` only, dead on arm64) and `mm/vmalloc.c:2793` (the `/proc/vmalloc`
debug print). The series' `VM_FLUSH_RESET_PERMS` is used at `baa585f67e0e:mm/vmalloc.c:2134,2150,2656`. Fusing them
means every `VM_FLUSH_RESET_PERMS` allocation also sets "tracking of direct mapped lowmem", which on arm64 turns
`VM_LOWMEM` into a lie in the debug print and would be checked by nothing. **Move `VM_LOWMEM` to `0x10`** — bit 4
is the only free one below `0x20`, and `0x10` is unused in both trees. Note the same header has `VM_KASAN 0x80`
in both, which is *also* dead on arm64, so a keep-both merge there is harmless.
`confidence: high` — three sdm670 use sites and three series use sites, all read.

**Row 5 — `SW_*` (new, uapi, most dangerous).** sdm670 has a Samsung-specific switch list
(`SW_HPHL_OVERCURRENT 0x10` … `SW_UNSUPPORT_INSERT 0x13`, `SW_GLOVE 0x20`, `SW_MAX 0x20`) used by
`sound/core/jack.c:43` (`jack_switch_types[]`). The series head replaces that whole block with upstream's
`SW_MACHINE_COVER 0x10`, `SW_MAX 0x10` and **no** `SW_GLOVE`, `SW_HPHL_OVERCURRENT`, `SW_HPHR_OVERCURRENT`,
`SW_MICROPHONE2_INSERT` or `SW_UNSUPPORT_INSERT`. Two separate breaks, both silent:
- `SW_HPHL_OVERCURRENT` and `SW_MACHINE_COVER` fuse at `0x10`.
- `SW_MAX` drops `0x20` → `0x10`, and `drivers/input/evdev.c:813` derives the evdev bit-array length from it.
  `snd_jack` is `=y` in both `*_eur_open` defconfigs, so `sound/core/jack.c` *is* compiled there and
  `jack_switch_types[]` will index out of a shortened array.
  **Suggested value: keep sdm670's `SW_*` block and add `SW_MACHINE_COVER` at a free index — `0x14` — rather than
  taking the series' list.** (`0x10` to `0x13` are sdm670's; `0x20` is `SW_GLOVE`.) The alternative, taking the
  series wholesale, deletes Samsung headphone-overcurrent reporting entirely, which is a product regression, not
  just a compile question. `INPUT_DEVICE_ID_SW_MAX` in `include/linux/mod_devicetable.h:294` follows `SW_MAX`
  (`0x20` → `0x10`) and must move with it or `INPUT_DEVICE_ID_SW` entries get dropped from the tables.
`confidence: high` — header contents, `jack.c`, `evdev.c` and the defconfig read directly.

**Row 6 — `KEY_*` (new, uapi).** `KEY_DUMMY_HOME 252` (sdm670) and `KEY_HOT 252` (series) both `252`, inside a
contiguous block where `253` (`KEY_WAKEUP_UNLOCK`) and `254` (`KEY_RECENT`) are **identical in both trees** —
so only this one slot changed hands. `KEY_DUMMY_HOME` has **zero** users anywhere in sdm670 (only its own define),
so renaming the slot is the safe fix: **use `0xff`** (code 255 is documented as reserved at
`input-event-codes.h:343`, so it is not free — the honest options are to keep `KEY_DUMMY_HOME 252` and give
`KEY_HOT` a new slot such as `0x300`, or accept the rename since nothing reads the old name). **`0xff` as the
script suggests is wrong for this row: 255 is reserved. Use `0x300`** (`KEY_MAX` is `0x2ff` in both trees, so
raising `KEY_MAX` too) **or drop `KEY_DUMMY_HOME`, which nothing uses.**
`confidence: high` on the collision and on `KEY_DUMMY_HOME` having no users; **medium** on the remedy, because I
did not check whether any out-of-tree Samsung userspace expects key 252.

### Same-enum renumbering (`ENUM_INSERT_RENUMBER`)

These are the same defect in a different shape: the series inserts a member into an existing enum, so every later
member changes value. No bit is double-booked, but sdm670's `kernel/bpf/verifier.c`, `net/core/filter.c` and
`kernel/trace/bpf_trace.c` (the only users of `ARG_PTR_TO_STACK` outside `bpf.h` @ `a30605a54f3b`) keep
referring to names whose numbers have moved. **Take the series side whole** — never merge these enums by hand.

`confidence: high` on the collisions and on `CONFIG_BPF=y` in all four; **medium** on the nl80211 remedy, because

## Method and coverage

`analysis/collisions/find_collisions.py` (Python 3, standard library only, deterministic — two runs produce a
byte-identical `K7.tsv`). It reads the kernel with `git show` / `git ls-tree` / `git cat-file --batch` only; it
never checks anything out and never writes to the kernel repo.

1. `git diff --name-only d54533f1546b baa585f67e0e -- '*.h'` → 367 headers.
2. Keep those present in **both** `a30605a54f3b` and `baa585f67e0e` → **224 examined**, **136 skipped** (absent
   from sdm670 — the Exy base has ARM/Samsung/vendor headers we do not), **7 skipped** (absent from the series
   head — files the series deleted). Both skip lists are counted on stdout so coverage is auditable.
3. From each tree's version of each header, extract `#define NAME <number>` (decimal, hex, `BIT(n)`, `1 << n`,
   `1UL << n`, with `(1 << n)` parens stripped), `enum` members with explicit *or implicit* values, and
   `struct`/`union` members with their declared type, array bound and bitfield width.
4. Report a collision when two **different** names exist only on their own side, share a value, share a prefix,
   sit in the **same `#if` environment**, and belong to the **same declaration family** — either two `#define`s
   within 60 lines of each other, or two members of the *same* `enum`. Same name with a different value is
   `VALUE_CHANGED`; a member whose type changed is `WIDTH_CHANGED`.
5. For each hit, resolve `CONFIG_*` guards against **all four** defconfigs through a small Kconfig model that
   follows `select`, `default` and `default ... if` — a defconfig grep is not enough (`LEAD-SYNTHESIS.md` §6.3).
   That resolver is what caught `CONFIG_EXT4_FS`: it is **absent** from `gts4lv_defconfig`, yet `CONFIG_EXT3_FS=y`
   at `gts4lv_defconfig:745` `select EXT4_FS` (`fs/ext4/Kconfig:7`), so ext4 *is* built in all four.

### Known limits of this scan

- Only headers the series **changes** are scanned. A collision cannot arise in an unchanged header, so this is
  complete for collisions, but a header the series does not touch can still be broken by a `.c` file it changes.
- Implicit `enum` values are inferred from declaration order, so an `enum` with an explicit out-of-order member
  would be mis-numbered. All 7 `ENUM_INSERT_RENUMBER` rows were verified by hand.
- The 60-line "same family" window is a heuristic. It correctly separated the six real collisions from the three
  `EXT4_` false positives, but a family written across a >60-line gap with a comment block would be missed. Each
  of the 46 rows was inspected, so nothing in the TSV is unreviewed.
- `WIDTH_CHANGED` normalises type spellings (`unsigned int` ≡ `__u32` ≡ `u32`, `struct X *` ≡ `ptr`) to avoid
  cosmetic noise. That normalisation is why the list is 14 rows rather than 4,810; the discarded rows were checked
  and were reordering (`__percpu *` vs `* __percpu`), `#ifdef` branches of the same member, or array-index
  spellings.
- MPTCP is confirmed off in all four defconfigs, so nothing here is inert for that reason — but I did not build
  anything, so "live" means "reachable from a `CONFIG_*` that resolves to `y`", not "compiled".

## Problems

- The script's automatic free-value suggestion for `KEY_HOT` (`0xff`) is **wrong**: code 255 is documented as
  reserved (`input-event-codes.h:343` @ `a30605a54f3b`). I overrode it to `0x300` in the table above. The script
  does not know about reserved ranges. **The lead should not trust any other `suggest=` value without reading the
  surrounding block** — the six real suggestions are all verified by hand above, but the script also emits
  suggestions for rows I judged lower priority.
- `nl80211_attrs` and `KEY_MAX` are uapi shared with userspace, which is outside the kernel repo. I can prove the
  kernel side of both; I cannot prove what the 22.2-era `wpa_supplicant` and input HAL expect. Row 5's remedy in
  particular assumes keeping the Samsung `SW_*` names is preferable, which is a product decision.
- I did not check whether any **out-of-tree** Samsung userspace or driver reads `KEY_DUMMY_HOME`. Inside sdm670 it
  has zero users (grepped the whole tree @ `a30605a54f3b`), but the input HAL lives in the device tree.
- `include/linux/bpf_verifier.h` `bpf_verifier_env.cur_state` changing from an embedded struct to a pointer is a
  large layout change; I report it because the type changed, but I did not audit its call sites.
